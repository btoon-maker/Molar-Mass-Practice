👋 Welcome

Molar mass is bookkeeping, not a fact to memorize. It's the sum of every atom's mass in a formula, and the atoms present depend on reading the formula correctly — including what a subscript outside a parenthesis does to everything inside it.

You won't need to memorize atomic masses here — they're provided. What you're practicing is turning a formula into an accurate count of atoms, then adding up their masses.

Your reasoning path

  1. Read the formula and identify every element present.
  2. Count each element's atoms — watch for subscripts outside parentheses, which multiply everything inside.
  3. Multiply each element's count by its atomic mass to get a subtotal.
  4. Add every subtotal to get the compound's molar mass.

The practice will tell you which element's count or subtotal to recheck if something's off, but it won't do the arithmetic for you.

Your practice set

Each session pulls 5 formulas from a bank of 20, mixing simple binary compounds, compounds that need parentheses, and one molecule with a lot of atoms to keep track of. Explain your thinking for each one; when all 5 are done, exporting a report is optional.
